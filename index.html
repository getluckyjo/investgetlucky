The product

Build the game Thursday night.
Settle it on the 18th green.

The Slip is your fourball's bet card — skins, nassau, dots, teams — sent to the group before the round. The app keeps the ledger live, nets the settlement, and carries the one thing no scorecard app on earth can: a Santam-insured jackpot up to $100,000 (FSP 3416).

Build the slip

Your four, your games, your stakes — under a minute. One tap rebuilds last week's.

Send it

The card lands in the group chat. Mates accept in one tap — nothing to download.

Play

The ledger runs live, hole by hole. Someone wins on every hole — that's the point.

The jackpot hole

One designated par-3, four filmed swings, an insured prize the whole group shares.

Settle

Netted to the fewest transfers: "Dave → you, $13. Done." Loser buys at the 19th.

Peer games — your money, your game

Skins with carryovers, nassau with presses, team better-ball, and dots — greenies, sandies, the snake — the micro-wins that pay someone on every hole. Closest-to-the-pin and longest drive too: the fourball confirms its own results.

We keep score and never touch the pot. No rake, no fee on peer games, no wallet — settlement deep-links to the payment apps your fourball already uses, and an unsettled bet carries to next Saturday. Every player's maximum exposure is shown before they accept the slip. Money is opt-in: Pride Mode — identical game, points only — is the default.

The recurring line

The subscription — $10/month for the app

Creating games needs a subscription; invited players ride free for the round and get the prompt after their first settlement. It buys the app — games, live ledger, handicaps, season history. It includes no swings and carries no prize risk. The category leader charges $40 a year for a fraction of this.

The house layer — the margin engine

The insured jackpot — group-pooled on the slip

The fourball buys in together on Thursday night: from $1 each for a $500 prize to $100 each at $100,000 — and if any of the four aces the designated hole, the group shares it. Four filmed swings on one staged hole, verified by us, paid by the insurer. Your three mates stop being camera operators and become co-investors in your swing.

$1 → $500 $5 → $2,500 $10 → $10,000 $50 → $50,000 $100 → $100,000

The insured challenge is skill-based and operator-funded — an entry fee to us, a prize paid by our insurer, no pot and no counterparty. On the 25 installed flagship holes the cameras verify instantly; anywhere else, film and submit — most aces confirmed inside 24 hours. Amateur odds: 1 in 12,500. ¹

Why the cover works — and who verifies what

Two kinds of money, two rules, never mixed. Peer money: the fourball attests its own results — one enters, one confirms — and we adjudicate nothing, hold nothing, take nothing. Insurer money: we verify every swing on camera, because our insurer pays the prize. The premium arrives with the swing that creates the risk, every time.

Five tiers from $1 to $100, a flat 1-in-12,500 per-attempt probability, 100% cover, 20% brokerage. Straight about the risk

The three questions we get asked first

Each one has an answer, and the answer is in the numbers rather than in the adjectives. The full risk register — including the ones without a clean answer yet — is in the dataroom.

What if Santam walks?

They sponsor R9m over three years and carry 100% of prize risk. Sponsorship is R2.5m of our R4.0m trailing revenue. If they did not renew we would lose that income and have to re-place the underwriting.

The answer

The prize-indemnity market has 20+ specialist carriers globally, and on the submitted terms this is a profitable book — a 31.9% loss ratio with 2.51× headroom. Re-placing it would cost us margin and time, not the business. We are targeting a second carrier on the panel before the global launch, the plan already holds SA sponsorship flat at R3.5m after the contract expires in 2028 — and Santam's written sign-off on the peer-game wrapper is a named gating milestone, not an assumption.

What if nobody adds the jackpot to their slip?

The jackpot is the margin engine, and its attach rate on a group slip — modelled at 35% — has never been observed, because nobody has ever offered this product.

The answer

We measure it before we finish building. The 90-day pilot on our 25 camera-rigged courses runs real slips with real jackpots and reads three numbers: slip creation, jackpot attach, average ticket. Above ~30% the plan is conservative; below ~15% we course-correct with the capital intact. And the downside is bounded: strip the jackpot to the sceptic case and subscriptions, courses, simulators and sponsorship still carry ~R38m of the R44.4m 2029 plan.

Isn't a betting app… gambling?

Two layers, two answers — and the structure was designed from the statute backwards, not defended after the fact.

The answer

The peer layer: private bets between players are lawful in South Africa and most US states precisely while no third party takes a fee or holds the money — so we take nothing and hold nothing, permanently. The app keeps score; your fourball settles the way it always has. The insured challenge: a skill competition with an entry fee to us and a prize funded by our insurer — no pot, no counterparty, no odds-based payout — with a written legal opinion in the dataroom. Before launch: a second counsel opinion covering the peer layer, and geo-gating per jurisdiction with Pride Mode (points only) as the default everywhere. If a market says no to money games, the product still works there.

And the one we get asked about most

What if someone actually wins? The insurer pays, and it is the best marketing day of the year. Every prize is pre-funded and 100% underwritten. The book is stress-tested for aces running 2.5× more often than amateur odds predict, for low-handicap players selecting into the top tiers, and for a full $100,000 claim landing in year one. It stays in profit through all three.

Straight answers

Investor FAQ

Is this gambling?

Two layers, two honest answers. The peer games are your own money — private bets between players are lawful precisely while no third party takes a fee or holds the pot, so we take nothing and hold nothing, permanently: the app keeps score and your fourball settles the way it always has. The insured challenge is a skill competition — an entry fee to us, a prize funded by our insurer, no pot and no counterparty — with a written legal opinion in the dataroom. A second opinion covering the peer layer is a named milestone before launch, and Pride Mode (points only) is the default in every market.

What if someone actually wins the $100,000?

The insurer pays. Every prize is 100% underwritten and verified by dual cameras plus an AI shot tracer. On the submitted terms the premium is $4.08 an entry against an expected claim of $1.30 — a 31.9% loss ratio, with aces needing to run 2.51× more often than amateur odds predict before the cover stops making money. A big winner is the best marketing day of the year.

How do the course partnerships work?

Courses earn 10% of every entry with zero upfront cost, on exclusive two-year auto-renewing agreements with restraint of trade; we cover installation, insurance, tech and ops. Metropolitan and Boschenmeer already run two holes each.

Who else does this? What's the moat?

Money-game apps exist — Skins App leads the category at $40/year flat, and 18Birdies (10m+ golfers) runs points-only side games. None of them can put an insured $100,000 on the slip — that takes an underwriter, actuarial credibility, a claims process and verification infrastructure, all of which we proved over 10,000 entries. The moat is the jackpot layer plus the Santam relationship plus 25 camera-rigged flagship courses — compounded by Ernie, who was 18Birdies' first official investor and chose to build this layer with us.

How does the money actually work?

Three layers. Peer games are free — skins, dots, nassau between your fourball; we never touch the pot and take no fee, and settlement deep-links to the payment apps you already use. The subscription — $10/month — buys the app: games, live ledger, handicaps, season history. Creating games needs one; invited players ride free for the round. The insured jackpot is bought per entry, group-pooled on the slip, with 24% of every entry ceded as premium on the same swing. Get Lucky earns from the subscription and the jackpot — never from your bets.

What exactly am I buying in this round?

15% of Get Lucky Golf Club (Pty) Ltd for R8m at a R53.3m post-money valuation (R45.3m pre), alongside the founders and founding investor Ernie Els. Minimum tickets from R1m. Full terms, the cap table and the valuation model are in the dataroom.

Isn't Ernie invested in a competitor?

He's a shareholder in 18Birdies — which is exactly the point. It proved golfers adopt round-companion apps at 10m+ scale, it runs its side games points-only, and it cannot underwrite a jackpot. We're the layer that category cannot build, he sits on both cap tables, and that makes 18Birdies a natural partner or acquirer rather than a threat. Complementary, not competing — and he backs this round with his own money.

How real is the simulator business?

The market is already built — we're monetising demand, not creating it. Simulators are a $2.4bn global market; South Korea alone logs ~94M rounds a year across ~9,000 venues, and Golfzon runs 6,500+ of them. Those machines already measure every shot, so there's no hardware and no installation on our side. What the venues lack is something to play for — an insured prize and the underwriting behind it, which is the slow part to build and the hard part to copy. Ernie opens those doors himself. R1.6m of this round builds the integration; the plan then takes 1.5% of one country's rounds by 2029 and counts no other market. Operator agreements are not yet signed; the dataroom sets out the adoption range.
