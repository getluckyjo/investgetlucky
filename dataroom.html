2 · The assumptions behind it

Launch base2,000 subscribers — the raise-funded launch converts them from the 60,000 members already reached
Subscription$10/month for a $10,000 insured prize per verified ace
Growth & churn10.5% → 8% → 7% gross monthly growth — slowed deliberately each year — with churn held at 4% (net 6.5% / 4% / 3%)
Gross margin66% — insurance premium 24% of revenue, CAC $12
Cost baseCosted to be delivered. Central cost is a stated budget that hires ahead of revenue — R9.5m (2027), R24m (2029), R46m (2032) — not a percentage of a revenue number. On top of it: payment processing at 3% of gross collections, verification and claims operations at $0.50 per paid entry, acquisition at $12 per gross new member, and brand and retention at 8% of revenue. Total cost R12.0m → R33.9m → R68.7m. Simulator EBITDA margin is 42% after integration engineering and operator marketing support.
FundingThe plan runs at a loss through the investment years — peak cumulative drawdown ~R5.0m across 2026–28, inside the R8m raise with headroom — and is profitable from 2029 without a further round.
Entry split (installed)66 / 10 / 24 — Get Lucky gross profit / course / Santam insurance, matching the 66% margin
Simulators — unit$1 entry for a $1,000 insured prize, built into the operator's software. Split 46 / 30 / 24 — Get Lucky / operator / insurer. The operator takes more than a course's 10% because it brings the venues, the software and the distribution, and Get Lucky installs no hardware.

In-play upsellsThe add-on menu — jackpot upgrade ($100 for a $100,000 shot), double-up ($5), three-for-two swings, group pot ($10 a head) — sold after the entry, at the moment of maximum intent. Modelled at 9% attach at $5 average on paid entries already in the plan; Get Lucky nets 55%. Attaches to attempts already in the model — it never creates new volume, so there is no double-count. Two further upsells — a nearest-the-pin prize and a highlight reel — are designed but parked until they can be delivered honestly: proximity cannot be measured with the current on-course rig, and there is no production pipeline for the clip. Neither is in these numbers.
Revenue multiple3.0× revenue, flat at every forward milestone. There is no listed comparable for an insured skill-prize platform — the closest structural analogue is a prize-indemnity MGA, which is valued on earnings, not revenue. So the multiple is set against what can be evidenced rather than borrowed: R4.0m traded, R9m contracted, and a plan costed line by line. The round itself implies 3.48× (R47.3m post ÷ R12.0m 2026 forecast revenue), so the multiple compresses as the company scales rather than expanding, and the milestones move only with revenue. At R39.3m pre this is roughly 11× trailing revenue — high for South Africa, and priced on the two contracts and the shareholder we have rather than the ones we want.
Territory sponsorshipSantam R9m over three years is signed (R2.5m → R3.0m → R3.5m) in a ~150k-golfer market. Each new territory appoints an anchor insurer partner on the same template — the underwriter of the prize is the natural sponsor of it. Modelled at US R2.0m/yr from 2029 rising to R4.0m, Europe R3.0m/yr from 2030, Japan R2.0m/yr from 2031: deliberately below the SA run-rate at entry, in markets many times the size. New-territory partners are appointed at market entry and are not yet in place.
