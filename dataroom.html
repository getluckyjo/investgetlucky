2 · The assumptions behind it

Launch base2,000 subscribers — the raise-funded launch converts them from the 60,000 members already reached
Subscription$10/month for a $10,000 insured prize per verified ace
Growth & churn10.5% → 8% → 7% gross monthly growth — slowed deliberately each year — with churn held at 4% (net 6.5% / 4% / 3%)
Gross margin66% — insurance premium 24% of revenue, CAC $12
Cost baseCosted to be delivered. Base opex R450k/month scaling at 0.68 with the subscriber base — a real team, four market entries, the simulator integration and the upsell build. Annual operating cost roughly doubles by 2029 (R5.4m → R15.2m → R27.1m). Per-stream EBITDA margins are struck after delivery cost: simulators 42% (integration engineering, operator marketing support), territory sponsorship 52% (local activation, partner servicing), upsells 60% (product build, payment costs).
FundingThe plan runs at a loss through the investment years — peak cumulative drawdown ~R5.0m across 2026–28, inside the R8m raise with headroom — and is profitable from 2029 without a further round.
Entry split (installed)66 / 10 / 24 — Get Lucky gross profit / course / Santam insurance, matching the 66% margin
Simulators — unit$1 entry for a $1,000 insured prize, built into the operator's software. Split 46 / 30 / 24 — Get Lucky / operator / insurer. The operator takes more than a course's 10% because it brings the venues, the software and the distribution, and Get Lucky installs no hardware.
Simulators — volumeAttach rate applied to ~94M annual simulator rounds in South Korea alone: 0.3% (2027) → 0.8% (2028) → 1.5% (2029) → 4.0% (2032). 2026 is an integration year with no revenue. Golfzon's venues outside Korea and every other operator are excluded.
Simulators — statusLive 2027. R1.6m of the raise (20%) funds the integration; Golfzon leads the target list and Ernie Els holds direct relationships with every operator on it. No operator agreement is signed as at August 2026 — commercial terms are those set out above, and the adoption range is modelled in table 3.
Simulators — margin50% EBITDA on Get Lucky's share — software-only, no cameras, no installation, no course revenue share
Prize riskExcluded throughout — every prize 100% underwritten by Santam (Authorised FSP 3416)
CurrencyFX 18.5 ZAR per USD; revenue = course entries + subscriptions + sponsorship + simulators
In-play upsellsThe add-on menu — jackpot upgrade ($100 for a $100,000 shot), double-up ($5), three-for-two swings, group pot ($10 a head) — sold after the entry, at the moment of maximum intent. Modelled at 9% attach on course and app attempts at $5 average and 4% at $2 in simulators, where the base entry is a dollar; Get Lucky nets 55% after course/operator share and insurance, at a 60% EBITDA margin. Attaches to attempts already in the model — it never creates new volume, so there is no double-count. Two further upsells — a nearest-the-pin prize and a highlight reel — are designed but parked until they can be delivered honestly: proximity cannot be measured with the current on-course rig, and there is no production pipeline for the clip. Neither is in these numbers.
Revenue multiple3.0× revenue, flat at every forward milestone. Engaged-community subscription businesses (Strava, Peloton, Whoop) trade at 5–10× revenue; 3.0× sits 40% below the bottom of that band, a discount for being private and pre-scale. The round itself implies 4.77× (R53.3m post ÷ R11.2m revenue), so the multiple compresses as the company scales rather than expanding — and the milestones move only with revenue. If the business were ever rated at the comp band, 2032 would be R673m–R1,346m against the R396m carried here; that is not in the plan.
The R1bn horizon outside the modelAt the same flat 3.0×, a R1bn valuation needs about R333m of revenue. Carrying the plan's own long-term growth rate (1.8% monthly — the same rate used to extend 2029 to 2032) past the model window, revenue passes that level a little over four years after 2032, so 2037. No new assumption is introduced. It sits outside the plan being underwritten: the 2032 milestone is R396m and that is what the deal is priced against.
Territory sponsorshipSantam R9m over three years is signed (R2.5m → R3.0m → R3.5m) in a ~150k-golfer market. Each new territory appoints an anchor insurer partner on the same template — the underwriter of the prize is the natural sponsor of it. Modelled at US R2.0m/yr from 2029 rising to R4.0m, Europe R3.0m/yr from 2030, Japan R2.0m/yr from 2031: deliberately below the SA run-rate at entry, in markets many times the size. New-territory partners are appointed at market entry and are not yet in place.
