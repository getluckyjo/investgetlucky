The spine the whole product hangs on

Two kinds of money, two regimes, never mixed. Every screen, flow and API in the specification is downstream of this table:

Whose moneyWho verifiesGet Lucky's role
The players' — skins, nassau, dots, teamsThe fourball: one enters, one confirmsNone. We keep score. We never touch the pot, hold nothing, adjudicate nothing — zero from peer games, permanently
The insurer's — the jackpotGet Lucky: filmed, verified, claims processFull verification, exactly as proven on the 25 installed courses

The subscription buys the app and only the app — games, ledger, handicaps, history. It includes no swings and carries zero prize risk: every insured swing is a separate paid entry, with 24% of that entry ceded as premium on the same swing that creates the risk. These aren't policies that can drift; they are written into the spec as constitutional requirements every build ticket cites.

Slip to settlement — the golfer's flow, specified end to end

The slipCourse, the four, handicaps pulled and overridable (the app never argues with the group), the game stack with per-game stakes, the jackpot layer — then send. Fifteen flows specified with steps, states, edge cases and acceptance criteria.
Guests join by linkAn invited player opens the slip in any browser — nothing to download, nothing to pay. Guests ride free, forever; the full slip and their own maximum exposure in plain currency are shown before they accept. Every slip is three product demos.
Lock at the first teeAfter lock, nothing about the accepted terms changes — no player's exposure can ever exceed the number they accepted. Late joins, drop-outs, no-shows and rained-off rounds all have specified resolutions.
ScoringOne enters, one confirms — every result carries two attestations from two different players. Disputes resolve inside the fourball by design; there is no referee desk, because the product's legal spine forbids one.
The live ledgerDerived, never hand-edited. Skins carry, presses arm, dots stack — and every line explains itself. Nobody argues on the 18th, because nobody can.
SettlementNetted to the provable minimum of transfers — "Dave → Johannes $13. Done." — deep-linked into the payment app each mate already uses. Get Lucky routes nothing and holds nothing; an unsettled balance carries forward as the reason the group returns next Saturday.

The engines under it

The rules engine is specified format by format — skins with carryovers, nassau with press caps, better-ball, the dots catalogue including the snake — as one pure, deterministic module: same slip, same results, same answer on every phone, with the worked examples shipping as the test suite. The handicap and fairness engine — named in the business plan as the make-or-break component — is specified to make every stroke explainable: sources labelled, allowances visible as presets, overrides always winning, the maths one tap away. The netting algorithm is provably minimal and checks itself to the cent before a settlement card ever renders.

The jackpot, verified

The ladder$1 → $500 · $5 → $2,500 · $10 → $10,000 · $50 → $50,000 · $100 → $100,000 — one tier per group ticket, 24% of every entry ceded as premium on the swing that creates the risk.
Group-pooledThe fourball buys in together at slip creation; any participating ace pays the group. Four swings on one designated hole — each a separately paid entry — so filming is a highlight, not an interruption.
Two verification lanesOn the 25 installed courses: the camera rig and shot tracer, near-instant, exactly as proven over 10,000 entries. On any other course: film-and-submit in-app — ball registered and marked, GPS-fenced, watermarked at capture — reviewed inside 24 hours against a published checklist.
Claim to payoutA specified state machine from verified ace to paid claim: evidence package assembled by the app, claim opened with the insurer through the broker, KYC in-app, and the prize paid by the insurer directly to the winners — never through Get Lucky, never from anyone's pocket.

Built to launch anywhere

PlatformWeb-first with wrapped iOS and Android builds from one codebase: guests need only a link, organisers get first-class push and camera. No second codebase, ever.
Offline-firstGolf courses have dead zones, so the full round — scoring, ledger, filming, arming — works with zero signal and syncs cleanly after. Conflicts resolve by attestation, never by clock.
Geo-gates from the first commitMarket × feature × mode flags are architecture, not an afterthought — a named gating milestone. Unknown market? The app defaults to points and no jackpot, automatically.
Pride Mode defaultThe identical game in points is the default state of every new slip and the compliant mode wherever real-money play is restricted — feature-complete, not a consolation.
Responsible play in v1Maximum exposure before every acceptance, table-stakes caps, self-set loss limits with cooling-off, "even it up" prompts — shipping in v1 regardless of what any regulator requires.

Measured before it ships — the pilot is designed, not improvised

The jackpot attach rate on a group slip is the number the whole model swings on — modelled at 35% of slips (20% sceptic / 55% bull) and never before observed, because nobody has offered this product. The 90-day pilot across the 25 installed courses measures it with the app's own instruments: the capture tool is the guest web slip, cut down, writing the same records and firing the same analytics events the app will fire — so the pilot's attach number and the app's attach number are the same measurement by construction, not a spreadsheet translated after the fact. Course staff log every fourball offered, so slip-creation share has a real denominator. Three numbers come out: what share of fourballs create a slip, what share of slips add the jackpot, and what they pay. Above ~30%, the plan is conservative. Below ~15%, the company course-corrects with the capital intact.

Open decisions — flagged, not fudged

The spec closes with a register of the decisions that belong to the founder, counsel or the insurer rather than to a build team — handicap-data licensing, the legal ownership and tax treatment of a group claim, the billing processor and app-store posture, pricing architecture across markets, data-retention periods. Each is named, owned and dated, with a holding position specified so the build never stalls waiting. A specification that pretended to settle these would be weaker, not stronger.
