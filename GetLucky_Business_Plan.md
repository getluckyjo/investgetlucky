# Get Lucky Golf Club — Business Plan

**A golf day in your pocket.**
August 2026 · Confidential

---

## 1. Executive summary

Get Lucky is building the app for the oldest game in golf: the money game
between mates. A fourball builds its bet slip before the round — skins,
nassau, dots, teams — sends it to the group, plays with a live ledger, and
settles on the 18th green. On top of that game sits the one thing no
scorekeeping app on earth can offer: **an insured jackpot, underwritten by
Santam, up to $100,000 on a single swing.**

The peer game is free, forever. Get Lucky never touches the pot and never
takes a cut — that is a legal necessity and a competitive weapon at the same
time. Revenue comes from a **$10/month subscription for the app** and from
**insured jackpot entries** bought per swing, at tiers from $1 → $500 to
$100 → $100,000.

The company is not starting from theory. Over twelve months it ran 10,000
paid challenge entries across 800 course activations, reached 60,000
members, installed camera-verification rigs on 25 premium courses, signed
R9m of Santam sponsorship, and put **Ernie Els on the cap table** — a
four-major champion who was also the **first official investor in
18Birdies**, the largest golf app in the category this plan enters, with
10m+ registered golfers. The proof of concept proved golfers engage. This
round builds the product they engage with daily.

**The raise: R8.0m for 15% (R45.3m pre / R53.3m post).** Ernie Els holds 5% non-diluting for name, likeness and network. Funds build the app (Cloud & Things, led by the
former CTO of Capitec Bank), run a 90-day live pilot across the 25 installed
courses, secure the legal and insurer sign-offs, and take the product to its
first paying markets.

## 2. Why the money game — the consumer evidence

Twelve months and 10,000 paid entries of proof of concept taught us exactly
what the product has to be, and the design principles are behavioural:

- **Reward every hole.** At amateur odds of 1-in-12,500, a hole-in-one is a
  rare event by design — the jackpot, not the game. A skins game with a
  dots layer pays somebody on **every hole**: 25–54 personal win events per
  player per year, the most reinforcing reward schedule in consumer
  products.
- **Make verification social.** The fourball is standing right there for
  every peer game. Filming is reserved for the one staged hole with
  $100,000 on it — a highlight of the round, not an interruption to it.
- **Sell on Thursday night.** The slip lands in the group chat when the
  weekend is being planned — the one moment social contagion sells for us,
  three playing partners at a time.

The insured hole-in-one takes its strongest position: the lottery ticket at
the till of a shop you were coming to anyway — with the fourball's whole
money game built around it.

The hole-in-one does not disappear — it gets a better job. It stops being
the reason to open the app and becomes the jackpot on the slip: the lottery
ticket at the till of a shop you were coming to anyway.

## 3. The product

### The Slip

The core object of the app, built in under a minute, designed to be
screenshotted into the group chat:

1. **The round** — course, date, tee time.
2. **The four** — from recents; one tap **"Rematch"** rebuilds last week's
   game entirely.
3. **Handicaps** — pulled automatically (WHS/GHIN), with every allowance
   visible and overridable. Every fourball has a house rule; the app must
   never argue with the group.
4. **The games** — a stack, each with its own stake: skins (gross/net,
   carryovers), nassau with presses, team better-ball, and **dots** —
   greenies, sandies, barkies, the snake — the micro-wins layer that
   guarantees the round pays someone on every hole.
5. **The jackpot** — the house layer, visually distinct: *"+$25,000 if
   anyone aces the 7th."* **Group-pooled: the four buy in together, and if
   any one of them holes it, the group shares the insured prize.**
6. **Send.** The card renders as an image in the group chat. Invited players
   see the full slip and accept in one tap — no download required. Each
   player's maximum exposure is stated in plain currency before they accept.
7. **Lock** at the first tee.

### The round and the 19th hole

A live ledger through the round — running P&L per player, hole by hole. On
the 18th: the **Settlement Card**, netted to the minimum transfers ("Dave →
Johannes $13. Done."), deep-linked to Venmo/Zelle in the US and
PayShap/Capitec Pay in South Africa. Get Lucky routes nothing and holds
nothing. An unsettled balance carries forward as an IOU — which is not a bug
but the strongest reason the group returns next Saturday.

### The jackpot, group-pooled

The design decision the whole product turns on: the fourball buys the
jackpot together at slip creation. Consequences:

- The three playing partners stop being reluctant camera operators and
  become **co-investors in the swing**.
- Filming collapses from 16 interruptions to **four swings on one staged
  hole** — a moment the group wants to film.
- The purchase becomes a group decision made in the excitement of building
  the slip, not a solo decision under pace-of-play pressure.

Mechanically the insured product is unchanged: entry per swing, tiers
$1 → $500 through $100 → $100,000, 24% of every entry ceded as premium on
the same swing that creates the risk, filmed and verified, prize paid by the
insurer. The published underwriting economics carry over exactly: **$17.00
weighted average entry, $4.08 premium, $1.30 expected claim, 31.9% loss
ratio, 2.51× breakeven** on ace frequency.

### Verification — two regimes, never mixed

| Whose money | Who verifies | Get Lucky's role |
|---|---|---|
| The players' (skins, dots, nassau, CTP, longest drive) | The fourball — one enters, one confirms | **None. We keep score. We never adjudicate, hold or touch peer money** |
| The insurer's (the jackpot) | Get Lucky — filmed, AI shot tracer, claims process | Full verification, exactly as proven on 25 installed courses |

This division is the product's legal spine as much as its operational one.
Closest-to-the-pin and longest drive ship as peer-verified games: the
company never needs to measure proximity, because the fourball is standing
on the green and can.

### The retention spine

The **Season Ledger**: a running lifetime P&L against each named mate.
*"You are $67 up on Dave since March."* No golfer deletes the app while he
is down to Dave. Around it: rematches, named groups with crests, an order of
merit, automated banter cards. Golf's oldest social loop, given a memory.

### Responsible play, from day one

Maximum exposure shown before every acceptance; table-stakes caps; self-set
loss limits with cooling-off; "even it up" prompts when a ledger gets
lopsided; and **Pride Mode — identical game, points instead of money — as
the default state**, which doubles as the compliant mode for any market
where real-money play is restricted.

### Launch posture

**USD-led, global, honour mode.** The full experience — slip, ledger,
settlement maths — works everywhere from day one because the app never
touches money. Wallets and custody are a later, market-by-market unlock
behind geo-gated feature flags, only where a licensed route exists.

## 4. Market

- **~160M golfers worldwide**; 48M in the US alone, where the money game
  (the $5 nassau, the skins game) is a cultural institution.
- The category's scale is proven: **18Birdies reports 10m+ golfers** on a
  free scoring app. Golfers demonstrably adopt round-companion apps in the
  tens of millions.
- The money game itself is universal and still analogue — played in every
  fourball, settled in the car park, tracked in chat groups. App attempts
  to date are track-only or points-only: the category is open.
- The prize-indemnity layer — Get Lucky's differentiated asset — sits on a
  **$1.2bn hole-in-one insurance market (2024 base)** with no consumer brand
  on top of it.
- South Africa (~150k affiliated golfers) is the proof market: 25 installed
  courses, the Santam relationship, the club network. The product's centre
  of gravity is global from launch.

## 6. Regulatory posture

Stated plainly, because investors will ask and the answer is good:

- **The peer layer is free and custody-free by design.** In South Africa,
  the National Gambling Act's informal-bet exemption holds only while no
  third party takes a fee or gain from the bet — so the moment a platform
  rakes a pot, the exemption dies. US state law reaches the same place:
  social-gambling exemptions prohibit third-party benefit, and precedent
  exists against a P2P exchange charging as little as 1%. **Get Lucky
  therefore takes nothing from peer games — not a rake, not a flat fee —
  anywhere, and models that revenue at zero permanently.**
- **The subscription is for the app** — GPS, handicaps, ledgers, history,
  group management — payable whether or not any money game is played. This
  is the fee structure with the strongest legal footing, and the copy and
  terms keep that framing clean.
- **The insured jackpot is a competition with an operator-funded, insured
  prize** — an entry fee to Get Lucky, a prize paid by Get Lucky's insurer,
  no pot and no counterparty. This is the structure the company has operated
  for a year and holds a written legal opinion on.
- **Gating milestones before public launch** (named, dated, investor-visible):
  1. Written counsel opinion on the P2P layer — specifically that the app
     subscription does not taint the peer-bet exemptions.
  2. **Santam's written sign-off** on the peer-game wrapper.
  3. Ernie Els's counsel sign-off on positioning and brand use.
  4. Geo-gating feature flags in the architecture from the first commit.
  5. The existing hole-in-one legal opinion published to the dataroom.
- Responsible-play controls ship in v1 regardless of what any regulator
  requires.

## 7. Business model & unit economics

Two revenue engines, one KPI engine:

| Line | What it is | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| **Subscription** | $10/month for the app. Organizer-pays: creating games requires a subscription; invited players get a full guest experience for the round, with the subscribe prompt after first settlement | The recurring line |
| **Insured jackpot** | Group-pooled entries at $1–$100 tiers, 66% retained after 24% premium and course share where applicable | The margin engine |
| **Peer GMV** | Money staked in peer games. **Revenue: zero, permanently.** Reported as the engagement KPI that drives both lines above | The growth engine |
| Sponsorship | Slips, settlement cards, the 19th-hole moment, order-of-merit boards — the richest sponsor inventory in golf apps. SA contracted at R3.5m run-rate, held flat; new territories at entry | Supporting |
| Simulators (2027+) | The slip inside operator software; machine-verified insured CTP formats (a market PinSeeker has validated) | Supporting |
| Installed courses | 25 flagship jackpot holes, instant camera verification, +25/year | Proof & brand |

### Per-active-player economics (monthly, USD)

| Input | Sceptic | Base | Bull |
|---|---|---|---|
| Money rounds per active player | 0.4 | 1.0 | 1.8 |
| Subscriber share of actives (organizer-pays) | 25% | 35% | 50% |
| Jackpot attach (% of slips) | 20% | 35% | 55% |
| Average jackpot ticket per player | $8 | $10 | $12 |
| **Subscription revenue / active** | $2.50 | $3.50 | $5.00 |
| **Jackpot revenue / active (net 66%)** | $0.42 | $2.31 | $7.84 |
| **Total / active / month** | **$2.92** | **$5.81** | **$12.84** |

Struck deliberately conservative — every input rests on behaviour that
already happens in every fourball on earth, plus one genuinely open
question (jackpot attach — see §8) that the 90-day pilot measures before
the app ships.

Everything around the ARPU is built to compound:

- **Churn**: group lock-in, the season ledger and unsettled IOUs defend a
  5–6%/month assumption — solitary consumer apps honestly run 10–15%.
  Group products roughly double the lifetime.
- **CAC**: every slip invites three golfers who experience the full product
  before paying anything. Blended acquisition cost falls toward the cost of
  acquiring organizers only.
- **LTV/CAC**: at base ($5.81 × 18-month life ≈ $105 LTV against the
  modelled $24 blended CAC) the ratio is ~4.4× — and every organizer's
  three invited guests push the blended cost down from there.

## 8. The one number that decides this business — and the 90-day pilot

Under this plan, most of the gross margin still comes from the insured
jackpot. The group slip makes that purchase far more likely — a group decision,
one staged hole, a shareable moment — but "more likely" is a hypothesis
until measured. **The jackpot attach rate on a group slip is the number the
whole model swings on.** We have modelled 20% / 35% / 55%. Nobody on earth
knows the true figure, because nobody has ever offered this product.

So we measure it before we finish building the app. **A 90-day manual pilot
across the 25 installed courses**: a WhatsApp bet slip, a form, a
spreadsheet, and the existing camera rigs. Three numbers come out: what
share of fourballs create a slip, what share of slips add the jackpot, and
what they pay. Above ~30% attach, this is a very good business. Below ~15%,
we will know it for the cost of a season's admin instead of an app build —
and course-correct with the capital intact. Investors are invited to hold us
to this milestone.

## 9. Go-to-market

1. **The pilot** (months 0–3): 25 courses, manual slips, real money on the
   jackpot through the existing verified product. Output: the attach rate,
   the first 1,000 fourballs, and a waiting list for the app.
2. **App launch** (Cloud & Things build, funded by this round): USD-led,
   global, honour mode, Pride Mode default. The invited-guest flow is the
   distribution strategy — every slip is three product demos.
3. **Club and society channel**: order-of-merit boards and society leagues
   give clubs a reason to promote it; the SA club network and Ernie's
   ecosystem open doors. The 25 flagship holes anchor launch events.
4. **Simulators** (from 2027): the slip inside simulator venues — four mates
   in a bay is the ideal money-game format — plus machine-verified insured
   CTP. Ernie's operator relationships are the route to market.
5. **Wallet markets** (later): only where licensed, behind feature flags,
   never load-bearing in the plan.
